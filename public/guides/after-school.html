Parent guide · Practical routines

Make space for the after-school reset

The end of the school day can be a transition, not an immediate start to another list.

1. Begin with a check-in

Offer a quiet moment and ask what kind of break would help. A child may want movement, company or less conversation.

2. Add the real commitments

Put pickups, appointments and dinner into the routine. Then look at the space between them before adding another activity.

3. Offer a small choice

Choose two activities that fit the time, supplies and supervision you have. A choice between two workable ideas is often clearer than an unlimited menu.

4. Make stopping predictable

Say when the activity needs to end and give a transition reminder. Choose an option that can be paused or put away in your available time.

5. Notice patterns without labels

Use practical notes such as prefers a quiet break before homework. Avoid treating one difficult afternoon as a fixed trait or a diagnosis.

General planning ideas for adults. Adapt to your child, supervision needs and professional advice where appropriate.
