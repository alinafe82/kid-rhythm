Parent guide · Practical routines

Create a quiet-time routine that fits

Quiet time can mean a calmer activity together or nearby. It does not have to mean silence or independent play.

1. Choose a realistic length

Start with a short block that fits the day. Adjust from experience rather than expecting a long break immediately.

2. Match the supervision you have

Use age-appropriate materials and stay available. A recommendation is an idea for a parent to assess, not a safety guarantee.

3. Set out a few supplies

Books, paper or a familiar building activity can make the start simpler. Keep any small parts or unsuitable materials out of reach.

4. Lower the transition effort

Use a consistent place or opening cue. Let a child know what happens after quiet time so the ending is less surprising.

5. Keep what works

Record brief feedback in the local planner. If an activity is frustrating or overstimulating, swap it; completing the plan is not the goal.

Plan your own routine

General planning ideas for adults. Adapt to your child, supervision needs and professional advice where appropriate.
